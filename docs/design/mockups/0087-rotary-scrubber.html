Rotary vs. a plain toggle

Same job — flip the track between Mutations and Snapshots — two ways. The rotary is one compact click-to-advance gadget (it's already how this app's theme switch works). The segmented control shows both options at once but eats more width. Both are wired to the card above.

A · Amiga rotary

← click to advance

For: no popover (dodges the zoom-anchored-dropdown problem), tiny, quiet, native to the app. Against: the other option hides behind a click — leans on the label + tooltip to stay discoverable.

B · Segmented

For: both options always visible, zero learning. Against: wider, and it competes with the compare toggle that already lives in the parked actions row — two segmented controls on one strip.

Three axes, one card

Why the rotary only unifies two of them: only two are timelines. Layer is a scope, not a time — so it stays its own control, and the snapshot track reads from it.

Layer
Which file you author — Series canon vs. a Book override. Not a timeline; kept as the Editing at selector. Restoring a snapshot writes this layer's file; inheritance re-folds downward exactly as a normal edit would.
Mutations
Story time — how the character's canon is rewritten through the manuscript by in-scene ⤳ markers. Read-only past states; position is the mode. Today it's a cramped rail widget — the rotary promotes it to the full-width foot.
Snapshots
Edit time — your own version history of this file, per layer. Notches cut into the edge so they read apart from mutation beads; Live sits at the right, restore captures first.
