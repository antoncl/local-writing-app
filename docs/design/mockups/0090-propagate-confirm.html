What the ADR fixes

  • A tick per candidate. Nothing is a review item until the writer keeps it.
  • Reasons visible. The detail line under every row says why it is here — the field, the marker, the quoted mention — in the vocabulary the app already uses (⤳ is the mutation mark).
  • The mention group folds. A hub character mentioned in sixteen places must not bury the five declared dependents. Folded shows the count; unfolding is one click; nothing in it is ticked by default.
  • The diff in view. The change the writer is propagating stays beside the list the whole time — was cool, now warm, the same colour rule as the snapshot compare.

What this mockup proposes (iterate here)

  • Three groups, not four routes. Rows group by tier — Declared, Markers on untouched fields, Mentions — because that is the order the writer reads in. The route is the detail line, not the heading.
  • Declared and untouched-marker rows start ticked; mentions start unticked. The default is what the routes already say about signal.
  • “All” / “none” per group, quiet text at the group header's right, for the sixteen-mentions case.
  • The stripe is the kind (lore violet, scene green); the kind word trails in caps for the screen reader and the colour-blind.
  • “Since” lives in the header. Default is the last propagation; the writer may pick any snapshot or the whole entry, and the right pane re-renders.
  • The primary says what it does: “Confirm 5 review items”, never a bare OK.

Open: which surface class?

Drawn here as a dialog-sized card. The surface taxonomy (design-language.md §4) makes modality the last resort, and a writer will want to glance at a candidate before deciding — which a modal forbids. The alternative is an editor tab (“Propagate Marek Vell”) in the perennial editor region, closable, so candidates can be opened beside it. This is the one decision the mockup cannot make alone.
