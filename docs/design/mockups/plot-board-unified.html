Plot board — unified model

A plotline is a plot-template instance — one concept. Templates are a palette (built-in, your own, or empty/ad-hoc); instantiating one spawns a plotline card that holds its beats. You drag a beat off a plotline card onto a story card; the story card wears that plotline's colour. Plotline cards are edited in place (view-designer §A). Everything here is a first-class board node → all of it is undoable.

What this mockup is asserting (react to any of it)

  • One concept. "Template instance" and "arc" are gone — there is a plotline, which is an instance of a plot template. The template list gains a + New template (roll-your-own), and an Empty tile for ad-hoc threads.
  • Plotline is a board node. A plotline card holds its beat list and is edited in place (rename, recolour, add/remove/reorder beats — no separate editor pane). Because it's a board node, its create / delete / edit are board commands → undoable for free (this is what dissolves the messy part of the undo design).
  • Two axes, not two homes. Story cards still live in their manuscript boxes (#874 lock); the plotline is the orthogonal colour + beats axis. You assign by dragging a beat from a plotline card onto a story card — the card then wears that plotline's colour and shows the beat badge.
  • See the threads (the payoff). Each beat shows a use-count — how many cards fulfil it (0 = a gap the structure exposes). And each plotline has a focus toggle: turned on for "Main plot" here, it lights the chain of cards fulfilling that thread's beats (the slate-outlined cards) and lets the rest recede. This is the "isolate one thread across the book" that neither the original nor master ever built.
  • Freely arranged. Everything is draggable — cards within their box (#874), plotline nodes anywhere. A columnar layout is a sensible default, not a rule. A card may carry beats from several plotlines ("Midpoint reversal" wears Main-plot + Romance); it keeps a primary for its own tint, the badges carry each beat's plotline colour.
